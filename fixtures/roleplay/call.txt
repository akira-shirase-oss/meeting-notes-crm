# A role-played sales call, written for this repository: two invented people at invented
# companies, read by two espeak-ng voices (scripts/make-roleplay.sh). No real person's voice
# or words. A is the seller (Northwind Software), B the buyer (Harbor Logistics).
# Lines: "A: text" or "B: text"; "[pause N]" is N seconds of silence (a screen share, a
# lookup); "#" starts a comment.
A: Good morning, thanks for making the time today. Can you hear me clearly?
B: Yes, loud and clear. Thanks for setting this up.
A: Great. I have us down for thirty minutes. I would like to cover where you are with the pilot, what is working, what is not, and what a full rollout would look like.
B: That works. I have a hard stop at the half hour, so let us keep it tight.
A: Understood. To start, can you remind me how many dispatchers used the pilot last month?
B: Twelve dispatchers across two depots. The north depot used it every day. The south depot used it maybe three days a week.
A: Why the difference between the two depots?
B: The south depot still runs a paper board for the morning shift. The shift lead there does not trust the screen yet.
A: That is common in the first month. What would make the shift lead trust it?
B: Honestly, seeing it be right for two weeks in a row. Last month the route estimates were off twice, and he remembers both times.
A: Do you know which days those were?
B: I think the fourth and the eighteenth. Both were days with road closures on the ring road.
A: That matches what our support team saw. The closure feed from the city arrived late on both days. We now poll it every five minutes instead of every hour.
B: When did that change go live?
A: Two weeks ago, on the first of the month. Since then the estimates have been within ten minutes on every route we can measure.
B: Good. I will want to see that in writing before I take it to the shift lead.
A: I will send you the report this afternoon. It breaks the accuracy down by depot and by day.
B: Please include the south depot separately. That is the one I need to win over.
A: Will do. Let us move to what is working. What has the north depot told you?
B: They like the handover notes. The night shift used to leave notes on a whiteboard. Now they type them in and the morning shift sees them on the route card.
A: Has that changed anything you can measure?
B: Missed pickups went down. We had nine missed pickups in the month before the pilot and three in the first month of the pilot.
A: That is a big drop. Do you think the notes caused it, or something else?
B: Mostly the notes. Two of the three misses were a driver who was new that week.
A: That is helpful. Would you be comfortable with us using that number in a case study, without your company name?
B: I would need to ask our legal team. Do not use it until I confirm.
A: Of course. Nothing goes out without your written approval.
[pause 150]
A: While I pull up the pricing sheet, can you tell me who else needs to sign off on a full rollout?
B: Our operations director signs anything over fifty thousand a year. Finance reviews it first.
A: And what does finance usually ask for?
B: A three year cost, the exit terms, and what happens to our data if we leave.
A: I can answer the data question now. You can export everything at any time, and when the contract ends we delete your data within thirty days and send you a certificate.
B: Thirty days is fine. Finance will want that clause in the contract itself, not just in a policy page.
A: That is reasonable. I will ask our legal team to put it in the order form.
B: Thank you. What about the three year cost?
A: For forty dispatchers across four depots, the list price is thirty eight thousand a year. With a three year term we can hold that price for all three years.
B: That is under the fifty thousand line, so it would not need the director.
A: It would not, but I would still suggest we brief her. A rollout touches every depot, and she will hear about it either way.
B: Fair point. She prefers short written briefings. One page, no slides.
A: I can draft that page. Would you review it before it goes to her?
B: Yes. Send it to me first and I will edit it.
A: Good. Now the harder part. What is not working?
B: Three things. The mobile app logs drivers out every night. The depot map is wrong at the south depot. And the reports take too long to load.
A: Let us take them one at a time. The logouts first. How often does that happen?
B: Every driver, every morning. They have to sign in again before the first stop. It costs maybe two minutes each, but they complain loudly.
A: That is a setting on our side. Sessions expire after twelve hours by default. We can raise it to seven days for your account.
B: Is that safe? These are shared phones in some cases.
A: For shared phones I would keep the shorter session and turn on the quick unlock code instead. A four digit code instead of a full password.
B: That sounds better. Can you turn that on for the pilot now?
A: I can ask support to enable it today. You would see it tomorrow morning.
B: Please do. That alone will make the drivers happier.
A: Noted. Second, the depot map at the south depot. What is wrong with it?
B: The loading bays are numbered from the wrong end. Bay one on the map is bay twelve on the ground.
A: That should be a quick fix. Can you send me a photo of the bay signs, or a sketch?
B: I will ask the south depot manager to send a photo this week.
A: Thank you. Once we have it, the fix takes about a day.
B: Good. And the reports?
A: Which reports are slow?
B: The monthly route summary. It takes almost a minute to open, and sometimes it times out.
A: How many routes are in a month for you?
B: About four thousand across both depots.
A: That is more than the report was built for. We have a new version that is precomputed overnight. It opens in a couple of seconds.
B: When is that available?
A: It is in beta now. I can add you to the beta if you are comfortable with that.
B: Only if the old report still works while we try the new one.
A: It does. Both stay available, and you can switch back at any time.
B: Then yes, add us.
[pause 75]
A: I have added you. You will get an email from our beta team with the details.
B: Thanks. Can we talk about training? If we roll out to four depots, I cannot be the one training everyone.
A: We include two remote training sessions per depot in the first month, and a train the trainer session for your shift leads.
B: Remote will not work for the drivers. Most of them never sit at a desk.
A: Then we can do one on site day per depot. That is an extra cost of one thousand two hundred per day.
B: Can you include the on site days in the first year price?
A: I can include two on site days at no cost if we sign the three year term. The other two would be at the daily rate.
B: I will take that to finance. They will ask for all four.
A: I understand. I cannot promise all four, but I will ask my manager.
B: Please do. Put it in writing either way.
A: I will. Let me check the calendar for on site dates.
[pause 120]
A: Our trainer is free the second and third week of next month. Would either of those work for the north depot?
B: The third week. The second week is our inventory count.
A: The third week it is, pending the contract.
B: What else do you need from me before you send the order form?
A: Three things. The number of dispatchers per depot, the start date you want, and the name of the person who will sign.
B: I can give you the start date now. We want to go live at the start of the next quarter.
A: That gives us about seven weeks. That is enough time if the order form is signed in the next three weeks.
B: That is tight with finance, but possible.
A: What usually slows finance down?
B: Security questionnaires. They will send you one with about two hundred questions.
A: We have a completed questionnaire on file, and our latest audit report. I can send both with the order form so they do not have to wait.
B: That would save at least a week.
A: Then I will include them. Is there a specific format finance prefers?
B: They prefer a spreadsheet for the questionnaire. The audit report can be a document.
A: Understood. Spreadsheet for the questions, document for the audit.
[pause 90]
B: One more thing. Our current contract with the old routing vendor ends at the end of the quarter. We cannot overlap more than two weeks.
A: That works with the plan. Go live at the start of the quarter, with the old system available as a fallback for two weeks.
B: And if the rollout slips?
A: Then we would delay your first invoice by the same amount. You would not pay for weeks you cannot use.
B: I like that. Put that in the order form too.
A: I will. Give me a moment to go through my notes, then let me read back what I have so far, so we agree on next steps.
[pause 120]
B: Go ahead.
A: First, I send the accuracy report today, with the south depot broken out separately.
B: Yes.
A: Second, support turns on the quick unlock code for the pilot today, so drivers see it tomorrow morning.
B: Correct.
A: Third, you ask the south depot manager for a photo of the bay signs this week, and we fix the map within a day of getting it.
B: Yes, I will ask him this afternoon.
A: Fourth, you are in the beta for the new monthly report, and the old one stays available.
B: Right.
A: Fifth, I ask my manager about all four on site training days, and confirm in writing either way.
B: Yes, and by when?
A: By the end of this week.
B: Good.
A: Sixth, I draft a one page briefing for your operations director and send it to you to edit first.
B: Yes. Plain language, no slides.
A: Seventh, I send the order form with the data deletion clause, the delayed invoice clause, the security questionnaire as a spreadsheet and the audit report, within two weeks.
B: Can you make it ten days? That gives finance more time.
A: Ten days it is.
B: And you still need the dispatcher numbers per depot and the signer.
A: Yes. Can you send those by next Tuesday?
B: I can send the numbers by Monday. The signer depends on finance. I will tell you by Tuesday.
A: Perfect. And you will confirm with legal whether we can use the missed pickup numbers in a case study.
B: Yes, but do not expect an answer quickly. Legal takes a while.
A: No rush on that one. It does not block anything.
[pause 60]
B: I think that covers it. Is there anything you need from me that we have not talked about?
A: Only one question. Is there anyone else, besides finance and your director, who could stop this?
B: Our IT team will want to review the phone setup. They manage the shared phones.
A: Would it help if our support lead met with them before the rollout?
B: Yes. Thirty minutes would be enough. I will find a time and send you an invite.
A: Thank you. I will make sure our support lead is free.
B: Great. Then I will look for the report this afternoon.
A: You will have it by four o'clock. Thanks again for your time, and for the honest feedback on the pilot.
B: Thanks. Talk soon.
